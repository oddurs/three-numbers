## Why it matters

The document that defines sRGB: its primaries, its white point, and — the
subject of Chapter 6 — its transfer function, including the piecewise form with
the linear segment near black that the "gamma 2.2" folklore omits. Where this
book and a secondary source disagree about what sRGB *is*, this decides it.

## Claims

- [high | §5.2] {#srgb-piecewise} The sRGB transfer function is piecewise: a linear segment of slope 12.92 below a linear value of 0.0031308, spliced to 1.055·L^(1/2.4) − 0.055 above it. #transfer
- [high | §5.2] {#srgb-breakpoint} The breakpoint sits at encoded value 0.04045, which is 8-bit code 10.3 — so the linear toe occupies eleven of the 256 available code values. #transfer
- [high | §Annex A] {#srgb-reference-display} sRGB is defined against a reference display and viewing environment, not in the abstract: 80 cd/m² white, a 20% surround reflectance, and an ambient of 64 lux. #transfer
- [medium | §5.2] {#srgb-not-2-2} The overall curve approximates but does not equal a 2.2 power law; the exponent of the non-linear segment is 1/2.4, and the piecewise construction exists to avoid the infinite slope a pure power law has at zero. #transfer
