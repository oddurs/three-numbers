## Why it matters

The reference for everything the tristimulus model leaves out: adaptation,
surround, the Hunt and Stevens effects, and the appearance models that try to
account for them. Chapters 8 and 13 depend on it.

## Claims

- [high | ch. 8] {#adaptation-is-gain} Chromatic adaptation is modelled as independent gain control on each cone class — von Kries's hypothesis — and this crude model accounts for most of the observed effect. #adaptation
- [high | ch. 9] {#sharpened-bases} The transforms used in practice (Bradford, CAT02, CAT16) use bases sharpened beyond physiological cone sensitivities because doing so predicts corresponding-colour data better. #adaptation
- [high | ch. 6] {#appearance-needs-context} A colour's appearance depends on the surround, the background and the adapting luminance; a tristimulus value alone does not determine appearance. #appearance
- [medium | ch. 16] {#cam16-replaces-cat02} CAT16 was introduced to fix CAT02's tendency to produce negative cone responses for highly saturated stimuli. #adaptation

## Notes
